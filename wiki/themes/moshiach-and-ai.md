# Moshiach &amp; AI

The Chassidic framing the group keeps returning to. Not a side-topic
— it's the lens through which several core members make sense of
their adoption.

## The Kinus framing

[chat 2025-11-14], half-jokingly:

> *"Everyone in the world is going to lose their jobs besides for
> shluchim."*

[chat 2025-11-14] extended seriously:

> *"Torah AI will educate your mekuravim … make you באין ערוך more
> in demand."*

[chat 2025-11-07] flagged a professor's Kinus talk as carrying *"major
giluyim"* — revelations on AI and Chassidus.

## The structural argument

The chat's working *hashkafa* on AI:

1. **AI collapses time and space.** Translation, transcription,
   information retrieval — these were *time*. AI removes them.
2. **The Rebbe spoke about technology** as a tool to spread
   *Yiddishkeit* — railroads, radio, satellites, internet.
3. **Removing constraints reveals essence.** When time/space friction
   drops, the *what to teach, who to reach, why it matters* — the
   irreducibly human part — becomes the bottleneck. Which is exactly
   the shliach's job.
4. **More mekuravim get educated → more shluchim are needed,** not
   fewer. AI makes Torah more accessible without removing the human
   relationship.

## The Bina Melachutit thread

[chat 2026-02-13] proposed the Chassidic
distinction: AI is *bina melachutit* — *artificial understanding*. It
processes; it doesn't have *chochma* (the spark of insight) or
*neshama* (soul).

[chat 2026-02-13] turned this into a full
Shabbos-table drosha (written by Gemini — the irony noted) tying
*Parshas Mishpatim's Eved Ivri* to AI's nature:

> *"In the modern world, everyone is talking about AI — Artificial
> Intelligence. But in the Holy Tongue, the term for this is **Bina
> Melachutit (בינה מלאכותית)**."*

Concluded:

> *"The machine has the 'bina,' but we have the 'neshama.'"*

Terminology dispute side-quest: *melachutit* vs. *melachit* — Hebrew
purists argued; nobody resolved.

## The skeptical voice

[chat 2026-02-15]:

> *"AI is ultimately limited by human imagination. Precision, judgment,
> and nuanced customization will always require people."*

This is the chat's *other* hashkafa: AI is a power tool, not a
prophet. Treat it accordingly.

## Where this lands in practice

The "shluchim as the irreplaceable layer" framing produces concrete
behaviors:

- **Curate the AI's input.** Don't trust raw model recall on Torah —
  feed it vetted sources.
- **Use AI for the time-friction tasks.** Translation, summary,
  transcription, layout — let it free *your* hours.
- **Reserve human time for the human work.** Relationships,
  *hadracha*, *farbrengen*, knowing your *baalei batim*.
- **Don't outsource neshama.** No AI-generated divrei Torah passed
  off as your own.
